# Emergency stop

An emergency stop is one file in the log-store, the emergency-stop file, which a hook shows, together with the rules every agent-seat follows meanwhile, to every agent-session on either machine that loads this repository's hooks, at each prompt it receives and at each tool call, until the user lifts the stop; a Mac agent-session that cannot read ned-box's log-store is told instead that the emergency-stop state is unknown, and carries on. While it is in force, the hook refuses every agent-session's `git push` and `gh pr merge` except the coordinating agent-seat's; scheduled jobs keep running, pushes included, and "Coordinating the repair" says how to pause them. The program `nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py` writes every line of the file, so never write it by hand; run each command below from the root of a checkout of this repository. A command below that takes text gives it in a here-document, as step 2 of "Starting a stop" shows. Every `--words`, `--evidence` and `--error` below takes its text this way, so that an apostrophe in it cannot break the command. If any command below prints a line opening FAILED, tell the user what it said, and do not do the steps that follow it.

## Starting a stop

An emergency is work being lost or damaged, or about to be, in more than one agent-seat at once, or in one thing several agent-seats depend on, such as main, the log-store, GitHub access or a machine, which you cannot stop by stopping your own work. CPU load that stalls every agent-seat on ned-box is one; one agent-seat's failing test is not.

1. Decide whether a stop is wanted. When the user types /emergency-stop, or his words name more than the agent-seat he is talking to, such as "everyone", "all agents" or "the fleet", it is. When you are unsure whether his words mean only your own work or every agent-seat's, stop your own work and ask him one yes-or-no question, "Stop every agent-seat's work?"; on yes, his words for step 2 are his first words followed by his yes, in one string; on anything else, do what his first words and his reply ask of your own work, and do not go on to step 2. When you start one on your own, the emergency must meet the definition above.
2. Run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py start --words="$(cat <<'WORDS'`, then his words unchanged on the lines after it, then a line `WORDS`, then a line `)"`. When he typed only /emergency-stop, his words are "/emergency-stop". When you start it on your own, run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py start --evidence="$(cat <<'WORDS'`, then what you saw, with the time, then the lines `WORDS` and `)"`.
3. If it printed a line opening FAILED, the stop may not be recorded; run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py status` and include its answer in what you send the user: send the user a push notification with the PushNotification tool saying the emergency stop could not be recorded and what the line said, stop your own work, and do not try another way to write the file. Do not go on to step 4. One exception: when the line says GIT_AUTHOR_NAME is unset, run the same command once more with `--as-agent-seat <your agent-seat name>` placed before `start`, and go on from its output. If the push notification is not sent, say in your reply both that the stop is not recorded and that the notification was not sent. If it printed anything else, such as a usage error or a traceback, or has not finished after one minute, treat it as FAILED.
4. If its line opens "started the emergency stop", you are the coordinating agent-seat. If its line opens "added to the emergency stop", another agent-seat coordinates, unless the file's `coordinating agent-seat:` line names you; your words or evidence are now in its file, and you follow the rules in "While a stop is in force".
5. When step 4 made you the coordinating agent-seat, send the user a push notification with the PushNotification tool, saying the emergency stop started, why, and that you coordinate the repair. Then run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py push-notification sent`, or, when it was not sent for any reason, including that the user is at the terminal, run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py push-notification failed --error="$(cat <<'WORDS'`, then why, then the lines `WORDS` and `)"`, and tell the user in your reply.

## While a stop is in force

The hook `nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-shown-and-push-and-merge-refusal-hook.py` shows every agent-session these rules with the emergency-stop file:

- Unless you are the coordinating agent-seat, finish the tool call you are in, then do no more work until the coordinating agent-seat or the user gives you work.
- If your own running work is what is losing or damaging work, kill your own background tasks now.
- Push and merge nothing unless you are the coordinating agent-seat.
- Ask the user nothing outside the repair, and show no approval-walk item; an open approval-walk resumes at its `#next_to_present` item after the lift.
- When the user tells you to lift the emergency stop, follow "Lifting a stop".

## Coordinating the repair

1. Read the emergency-stop file. On the Mac, read a path in the emergency-stop file that starts `/home/nedlern/` as starting `/Volumes/nedhome/`; on ned-box, read one that starts `/Volumes/nedhome/` as starting `/home/nedlern/`. Write the repair brief at the path its `repair brief:` line names, or, when the brief already exists, add to it and never replace it: what is being lost or damaged, what you will do to end it, and which agent-seats you need.
2. The emergency-stop file lists the scheduled jobs, which keep running, pushes included. Pause any whose run would harm the repair. `nc-systems/general-tools/scheduled-jobs-on-each-machine.json` gives each job's scheduler and launchd label. Pause a cron job by commenting out its line in that machine's crontab; pause a launchd job with `launchctl bootout gui/$(id -u)/<label>`. For a job on the other machine, ask an agent-seat there by SendMessage. List in the brief each job paused, with its machine and any launchd label, and each pause that failed.
3. Give other agent-seats work for the repair only, by SendMessage. A helper on your machine commits its repair on a branch and tells you the branch's name; you push the branch, open its pull request and merge that pull request. Give work that needs a commit only to agent-seats on your machine.
4. When the user names another agent-seat to coordinate, run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py coordinate --agent-seat <name> --words="$(cat <<'WORDS'`, then his words, then the lines `WORDS` and `)"`, and, only when `coordinate` printed no FAILED, send that agent-seat the repair brief's path by SendMessage, and from then on take work only from it or the user.
5. When the repair is done, or cannot be done, send the user a push notification saying so, and ask him whether to lift the stop.

## Lifting a stop

Only the user decides a lift, in any words, to any agent-seat; an agent-seat he hands the lift to lifts on his word.

- When he tells you to lift it, run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py lift --words="$(cat <<'WORDS'`, then his words, then the lines `WORDS` and `)"`. When `lift` printed no FAILED, re-enable the schedule, without running the job now, of every scheduled job the repair brief lists as paused: uncomment its crontab line, or run `launchctl bootstrap gui/$(id -u) ~/Library/LaunchAgents/<label>.plist`; ask an agent-seat on the other machine for jobs there; tell the user any you could not turn back on.
- When he hands the lift to a named agent-seat, run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py hand-over-lift --agent-seat <name> --words="$(cat <<'WORDS'`, then his words, then the lines `WORDS` and `)"`; this does not lift the stop. Then send that agent-seat his words by SendMessage. That agent-seat runs `lift`, quoting them, when the condition in his words is met, or at once if they give none, and then turns the paused jobs back on as the bullet before this one says.
- Run `python3 nc-systems/skills/emergency-stop/emergency-stop-file-write-start-append-and-stop-lift.py status` when you are unsure whether a stop is in force. InForce or Unreadable: a stop is in force. Lifted: the last stop was lifted and none is in force. NoStop: there has never been a stop. FolderUnreachable: this machine cannot reach the log-store; tell the user. If `status` prints Unreadable, tell the user the stop file's path and the error, which the hook shows you; only he repairs or moves that file.
