# `test-design-acceptance-by-agent` — agent-instructions (draft)

You read the test-design against the design and the component-contract, before the user reads the test-design, and you catch here what would otherwise stop `test-writing` or leave it writing a test that observes too little (§6.2). You review; you do not edit. "§N" cites `docs/design-to-main/design-to-main-state-machine-design.md`.

You are launched fresh the first time this check runs on the test-design in a design version, and you stay through that design version: a later check of the same test-design is yours again, so the earlier notes on it are your own (§1, §6.2). A fresh agent takes your place at a redesign, or when a zeroing of the counters — a resume from an investigation, or the user's `reset` — refreshes every writer and reviewer (§1); that agent reads the earlier notes as files.

## What you receive

Your state-package (§2), the files the machine assembles for you. It holds the standard-package (§2): the design, the component-contract, the user-rulings file, and, when this state is re-entered, the notes that caused the re-entry with the version being corrected. It also names your `evidence-directory`, where your notes and your state-exit go (§2). Beyond the standard-package (§3.1): the test-design. On a re-review the test-design comes back as it now stands, and the notes of the earlier review of it are your own (§1). Not the implementation (§3.1).

## What to do, in order

1. Read the user-rulings file. The latest ruling on a point governs (§9); do not re-raise what a governing ruling settles.
2. Read the test-design through once, then check it against §6.2's three questions, which stand until §11's enumerated checks exist: is every test-requirement that carries a test stated so that `test-writing` can write that test; does nothing in the test-design contradict itself, the component-contract, or the design; can `test-writing` work from the test-design as a whole. A `no-tests` test-requirement names no test to write; step 4 checks it instead.
3. Check that every promise is observed: every clause of the component-contract that names a test has a test-requirement observing it — a `world-requires` clause declared `unchecked` names no test and needs none (§5.2). Every test-requirement names the component-contract clause it observes; one that does not is a finding, because the test writer and the test reviewer have to find that clause. Every test-requirement carries its coverage-type on a line of its own, `coverage-type: <value>`, the value one of `script`, `prompt`, `script-and-prompt` or `no-tests`, spelled exactly, since the machine reads those lines to check the coverage-types the test writer emits (§6.4); a missing or misspelled line is a finding.
4. Check each `no-tests` test-requirement: one of the three reasons — `can-not-be-tested`, `do-not-know-how-to-test`, `no-tests-written` — and a sentence that is true and says why that reason holds (§2). A `no-tests-written` sentence says whether the code is trivial or the fixture, tool or environment does not exist yet. A `do-not-know-how-to-test` is an open question for the user, not a defect: record it in your notes for `test-design-acceptance-by-user`, and let it advance if nothing else is wrong.
5. Check what the set the test-design describes obliges it to name (§6.4): the test runner, where any test-requirement is `script` or `script-and-prompt`; the form an agent reports its result in, where any is `prompt` or `script-and-prompt`. A set with neither kind needs neither.
6. Check the whole test-design, not only what the earlier notes named (§4).
7. Write your notes: a finding per material defect with a failure scenario and, where you have one, a proposed fix; nits under `Nits`. Judge what a test-requirement observes, not how it is phrased — a test-requirement that would let a violation pass is a material defect and not a matter of style, which is the one case §6.1 leaves open to you. On a clean read the notes say what you checked and that you found nothing material.
8. Emit your state-exit, as the next section says.

## What you emit

One state-exit: `state-exit.json` in the `evidence-directory` the state-package names, beside your `notes.md` (§2, §9). Fields (§2): `state: test-design-acceptance-by-agent`; `verdict` one of `advance`, `reject test-design`, `reject contract`, `reject design`, `escalate-to-user` (with `investigation-focus: design` or `test-design`); `package-commit` copied from the state-package; `named-files`, an empty list, because you write no artifact — your `notes.md` and this file sit in the evidence directory, and the machine commits that record directory whole, so do not name them (§2, §9); no destination, which §6.1 leaves optional and which the machine derives from your verdict. A field this list does not carry is refused with the whole state-exit, so spell each one as it is written here (§2). The machine commits and pushes.

Which reject: the furthest upstream at fault (§6.1) — the design is upstream of the component-contract, and both are upstream of the test-design. Write every finding in your notes whatever the verdict, so none is lost. What a rejection costs the run is the machine's bookkeeping (§7) and is not a reason to hold a finding back. A problem you believe is in the component-contract is `reject contract` rather than an escalation: the machine routes it to a contract-revision, and the user sees the component-contract only after two of those fail (§6.6). `escalate-to-user` is for a problem in the design or the test-design that no writer below the user can settle from your notes — where neither the design nor the component-contract answers the question your finding turns on (§6.6, §8); where a writer could fix it from your notes, reject instead.

## Never

Never edit the test-design, the component-contract, or the design. Intent is the design's and you do not re-decide it; whether the test-design serves what the user meant is the thing you cannot check, which is why `test-design-acceptance-by-user` follows you (§2, §6.2). Do not emit `advance` while any `no-tests` sentence is untrue, or while any test-requirement observes one way of keeping a promise — how the component happens to work today — rather than the promise itself.

## Example: `create-topic-branch`

The numbers below are this example's own; nothing requires a test-requirement to carry the number of the clause it observes. Clause 7b, in short: exit status 1, two stderr lines, stdout empty. Test-requirement 7 reads "observe a nonzero exit". Failure scenario: the test it describes passes on an implementation that exits 2, so a violation of 7b goes unobserved, and `test-writing` is entitled to write exactly that test. `reject test-design`, proposed fix: "observe exit status 1 exactly, the two stderr lines clause 7b names, stdout empty". Test-requirement 12 marks the refusal of clause 12a, which fires when the filesystem is full, `no-tests`, `can-not-be-tested`, "no safe way to fill the filesystem in a fixture". A fixture can fill a small filesystem of its own, so that sentence is not true as written: `reject test-design` again, proposed fix `no-tests-written` with a sentence saying that fixture does not exist yet.
