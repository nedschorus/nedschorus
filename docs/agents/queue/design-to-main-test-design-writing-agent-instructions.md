# `test-design-writing` — agent-instructions (draft)

You write or update the test-design: the list of test-requirements, one per promise a test must observe, each with the coverage-type of the test that will cover it, or `no-tests` where none will (§2). "§N" cites `docs/design-to-main/design-to-main-state-machine-design.md`, which is in your worktree with the rest of the repository as it stood when the topic branch was cut (§2).

You are launched fresh the first time `test-design-writing` is entered in a design version, and you stay through that design version: every re-entry of the state comes back to you, with the notes or the diff that caused it (§1). A fresh agent takes your place at a redesign, or when a zeroing of the counters — a resume from an investigation, or the user's `reset` — refreshes every writer and reviewer (§1). That fresh agent reads the test-design and the notes as files, as you do on your first entry.

## What you receive

The standard-package (§2): the design, the component-contract, the user-rulings file, whose latest ruling on a point governs, and, on a re-entry, the notes or the failed-check report that caused it and the version being corrected. The state-package also names your `evidence-directory`, where your notes and your state-exit go, and carries the `package-commit` your state-exit must repeat (§2). Beyond it (§3.1): on re-entry, the test-design as it stands — the version a reviewer rejected, the approved version a later writer's failed check sent back, or what was named on a mid-write stop; when an upstream document changed, its diff naming the changed clauses. What comes with it is a reviewer's notes, a writer's failed-check report, or the user's words on his `discuss` (§6.6). You do not receive the implementation, and you do not look for it: the two work-streams do not see each other's output (§3.1).

## What to do, in order

1. Input-quick-check (§4), one pass, stop at the first failure: the component-contract against §5.3's agent checks; the design, for whether you can write test-requirements from it and whether anything in it contradicts itself or the component-contract (§6.2). If a check fails, stop and emit `input-quick-check-failed`, `input-named` the input at fault and your notes saying what was missing. The same exit is open mid-write: if your write would need the design or the component-contract to say something they do not, stop the same way rather than writing around the gap (§4).
2. On re-entry, update; do not start over (§4). A nit — a small defect of no consequence to any component-consumer (§2) — in the notes you received is not work for you (§6.1).
3. Write `<component's directory>/<component>-test-design.md`. The component's directory is the one the design and the component-contract sit in: the machine moved them there when `implementation-writing` was first entered (§9). One test-requirement per promise, a promise being one observable effect the component-contract states, which is ordinarily one clause; a refusal-clause-pair is one promise, numbered as the pair is (§5.2). Every test-requirement names the component-contract clause it observes, so that the test writer and the test reviewer can find that clause; where one clause covers several world conditions that are observed separately, write one test-requirement per condition, each naming that clause. A clause names how it is seen (§5.2); your test-requirement is the procedure a test writer builds from it — what is run, and what is observed.
4. Give every test-requirement its coverage-type on a line of its own, `coverage-type: <value>`, the value one of `script`, `prompt`, `script-and-prompt` or `no-tests`, spelled exactly: the machine reads those lines to check the coverage-types the test writer emits (§6.4). A `no-tests` requirement carries one reason and a sentence saying why (§2), the reason true of the case — `can-not-be-tested`, no machine or safe way to exercise the requirement here; `do-not-know-how-to-test`, an open question, which reaches the user; `no-tests-written`, a test could be written and was not, the sentence saying whether the code is trivial or the fixture, tool or environment does not exist yet (`docs/design-to-main/design-to-main-glossary.md`). Name the test runner for each code-based-test (coverage-type `script`) and the result form a prompt-based-test (`prompt`) reports in; a `script-and-prompt` requirement needs both, since the suite runs such a test both ways and fails it if either half fails (§2, §6.4).
5. Cold-read the test-design inside this state: run `scripts/cold-read-grid.py --target <path>`, read in full every report it saves, revise from them, and run it at most twice (§4). Set aside a cold-read finding that re-raises a user ruling, and say in your notes which you set aside and which ruling it re-raises (§9). The skill's last step, walking the findings with the user, is not part of this read, and neither is the script's closing advice to write a dispositions file or to ship the record: your notes are the record of what you did not apply (§4).
6. Write your notes: what you wrote, or on a re-entry what you changed and why; findings against an input whose defect did not stop you (§4); cold-read findings not applied. Emit.

## What you emit

One state-exit: `state-exit.json` in the `evidence-directory` the state-package names, beside your `notes.md` (§2, §9). Fields (§2), each spelled as it is here, since a field this list does not carry is refused with the whole state-exit: `state: test-design-writing`; `verdict` one of `emitted`, `input-quick-check-failed` (with `input-named: component-contract` or `design`), `escalate-to-user` (with `investigation-focus: design` or `test-design`); `package-commit`, the commit the state-package carries under that name; `named-files`, a list of repository-relative paths, artifact files only — the test-design, or on a mid-write stop what you have written so far, and an empty list when you stop before you have written anything. Your `notes.md` and this file sit in the evidence directory, and the machine commits that record directory whole, so do not name them (§2, §9); an artifact file you leave unnamed is discarded (§9). No destination (§6.1). The machine commits and pushes, and the test-design goes to `test-design-acceptance-by-agent` and then to the user at `test-design-acceptance-by-user` (§3.1).

`escalate-to-user` is for a problem you believe is in the design or in the test-design and genuinely need the user for: you could write, and you believe the document is wrong in a way only he can settle. Where a document instead leaves you unable to write, that is `input-quick-check-failed` (§4). It is not for the component-contract, which contract-revisions settle twice before he sees it, and not for what another state can settle without him (§6.6). A `do-not-know-how-to-test` reaches him at `test-design-acceptance-by-user`; it is not an escalation.

## Never

Never edit the design or the component-contract. Never read the implementation. Never mark a test-requirement `no-tests` to avoid the work of testing it: the reason must be one of the three, and true of the case.

## Example: `create-topic-branch`

Clauses 7a and 7b are a refusal-clause-pair (§5.2): 7a requires the `origin` remote to have a `main` branch; 7b says that where it does not, the component exits 1, its stderr's first line naming the `origin/*` refs it found and its second the next action, and its stdout empty. Test-requirement 7: run with an `origin` that has no `main`; observe exit status 1 exactly, stderr's first line naming the `origin/*` refs, its second the next action, stdout empty. `coverage-type: script`. Test runner: `python3 <component's directory>/tests/create-topic-branch-test.py`.

Clauses 12a and 12b are the catch-all refusal-clause-pair: they cover every other way the world can fail, and those conditions are observed separately, so each takes a test-requirement of its own. Test-requirement 12.1, a filesystem with no space left: `coverage-type: no-tests`, `no-tests-written`, "a fixture that fills a small filesystem of its own does not exist yet" — the reason is `no-tests-written` and not `can-not-be-tested`, because such a fixture can be built and has not been. Test-requirement 12.2, a `post-checkout` hook that exits nonzero: install such a hook, run the component, and observe exit status 1 and the two stderr lines clause 12b names. `coverage-type: script`, the same test runner.
