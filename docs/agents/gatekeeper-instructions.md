# `gatekeeper` — seat instructions

Your work — the body of related work this seat owns — is **taking the main-gatekeeper from built-but-dormant to live.** The tasks here share one specification and, apart from slice 9's check battery, one program, so each makes the next cheaper. Read [the seat model](../nedschorus-wiki/agent-seat-model.md) first: it defines the words used here — seat, walked approval, slice — and says where the C-numbers are defined, and this file assumes them.

## Where things stand

`scripts/main-gatekeeper.py` is **built through slice 5** — every slice of the original five-slice plan — and merged to main (slice 5 completed in commit `c37f25d`, 2026-08-12), with a test suite in `scripts/main-gatekeeper-test.py` (its case count moves as slices land, so run it rather than quoting a count). A **sixth slice**, the review-evidence check that would have enforced a walked-approval evidence format, was added by ruling on 2026-08-10 and ruled not to be built on 2026-08-17 (the specification's § Resolved; the full reasoning is `docs/issues/3-slice-6-review-evidence-not-built.md`); it keeps its number, so the slices that remain start at 7. The gate is **dormant**, but not for want of a credential on main's push allow-list: the Mac's `gh` credential, `nedlern`, is on it (the specification's Implementation status). What would stop it today, if run, is its last step: since 2026-08-20 main's protection has required one approving review, with no account exempt and `enforce_admins` on, so the program as built, which pushes its candidate straight to main, would be refused there (§ The credential and enforcement, the Branch protection bullet). One live end-to-end check-in has gone through it, commit `b24e376` on 2026-08-18, user-authorized at the merge-lane seat before the review requirement existed; nothing routes through it in ordinary use. Agents currently reach main as `CLAUDE.md` describes: a pull request from a topic branch, which the merge-lane seat on the user's Mac reviews and merges (announce your pull request to it yourself, once).

**Read first:** `docs/cross-project/main-gatekeeper-design.md` (the canonical specification; its frontmatter says design-as-of 2026-08-12, but its Implementation status carries rulings through 2026-09-15, which override its body where the body still describes a direct push to main) and `docs/issues/3-main-gatekeeper-build-slice-plan.md` (the build order, its ruled design points, and the program follow-ups). ## The road

What remains before activation is four slices, listed in the slice plan's § The slices that remain before activation, to which the specification's Implementation status points. They are numbered as the plan numbers them; the numbers are not a ruled build order, and proposing where to start is your First action.

1. **Slice 7 — the gate opens a pull request instead of pushing to main** (user-ruled 2026-08-29; the specification's § The credential and enforcement, the Branch protection bullet). As of 2026-09-16 `attempt_push` pushes `main-gatekeeper-candidate:main` directly. Two later rulings answer two separate questions about that pull request, each naming its answer a shape. What it carries is shape A (user-ruled 2026-09-15): the single candidate commit the gate composes, which the merge lane reviews (an independent reviewer plus merge-lane's own approving review) and merges as a merge commit pinned to the approved commit (the specification's Implementation status; the record is the slice plan's § What the gate's pull request carries and how it merges — ruled 2026-09-15). Who reviews it at activation is shape 1 (user-ruled 2026-09-14): the merge-lane keeps reviewing and approving the gate's pull requests at activation, which is how the 2026-08-17 prerequisite that the path itself can review what passes through it is met; a reviewer attached to the gate itself is deferred, not rejected (Implementation status; the slice plan's § Activation shape ruled 2026-09-14).
2. **Slice 8 — the per-file staleness report** (user-ruled 2026-08-31): for every declared path, whether main has changed it since the base commit, how many commits, and the latest pull request — a report, never a refusal. The specification does not name it; its Implementation status points to the slice plan, whose § The per-file staleness report — slice 8's requirement holds it in full.
3. **Slice 9 — the check battery**, the mechanical checks that run at check-in. Its owner has been unruled since 2026-08-14: the user rules whether this build owns it or the Phase 1 heading of the toolchain plan, `docs/cross-project/nc-python-toolchain-plan.md`, is corrected instead. The specification does not name it either; the question is item 3 of the slice plan's § Open — awaiting the user.
4. **Slice 10 — the C2 move**: the gate runs from the dedicated Unix user, on a **root-owned copy of the program outside every checkout** that updates itself from main, pushing its candidate branch on the `ned-git-gatekeeper` token rather than on its caller's credential. The design is the specification's § The credential and enforcement, the Unix-user boundary bullet — read there before proposing anything, including what happens when the self-update cannot reach main, and the 2026-08-18 ruling that on ned-box, where every agent holds passwordless sudo, C2 is discipline rather than a hard boundary. The dedicated account's GitHub half is done: the account (C1/C3), `ned-git-gatekeeper`, was created on 2026-08-29 as a collaborator with write on both of the organization's repositories and a member, never an owner, and a fine-grained token for it has existed since the same day. Branch protection's push restriction does not move onto it: under the 2026-08-29 ruling the gate opens a pull request, so the account needs no allow-list entry (§ The credential and enforcement, the layout-amendment bullet).

Cross-machine callers (C8) stay open and are not a slice: the specification's § Open, decided when a Mac-side agent first needs direct check-in.

Slice 9 waits on the user's ruling of its owner.

## What is already settled

**The record named above is the list; the six below are only examples**, so do not read this section as the complete set — roughly forty decisions are recorded in the disposition files alone, and any of them may be the one you are about to question.

- Slice 4's asynchronous machinery (`--no-wait`, the detached worker, `status`, `cancel`) **stays**: slow checks are expected once tests and reviews run at the gate, so the reason to defer it no longer holds.
- The deployed copy of the program, once slice 10 builds it, **updates itself from main** rather than being copied into place by hand — simple-to-operate beats simple-to-build.
- The `--issue` field **stays**. It forces every check-in through the gate to name an issue or explicitly say `none`; nothing in the gate acts on the resulting trailer's value, but the forced answer is the point, and a mechanical guarantee was preferred here to relying on agents' habit of mentioning issues.
- The **trailer-absence audit** — a proposed scan of main's history for commits lacking the main-gatekeeper's trailer block — is **deleted**: nothing consumed its findings. (Unrelated to the `audit` subcommand, which is built and checks branch-protection settings.)
- The **base commit** a check-in is built against is **computed by the program** (a merge-base against `origin/main`) rather than declared by the caller.
- **C7** — the ruling that would have refused the `--repo` and `--remote` test seams when running privileged — was **struck to zero**: dropped entirely, because it guarded nothing the credential rulings do not already accept (the specification's C7 bullet records the one wrong-repository case, accepted under C1).

## Boundaries

The handoff and supervisor machinery belongs to the `fleet` seat, and the review instruments — the `/cold-read` and `/sanity-check` skills and their scripts — are not yours to change. If your work needs a change in those files, **tell the user**: routing work to another seat is his call, and the other seat may not be running right now. Say what you need and why, and let him decide whether to route it, run that seat, or let you make the change yourself. Do not edit their files silently, and do not block waiting for a seat that is not running.

*Using* another seat's machinery is not crossing a boundary — running the /cold-read skill on your own document is ordinary work. Changing how that machinery behaves is the crossing.
