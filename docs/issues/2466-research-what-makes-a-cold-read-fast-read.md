## Questions to answer

1. **Where the time goes.** Split a fast read's time into its parts, and say which part is the roughly 2 minutes of fixed cost: the reader CLI's start-up and sign-in, sandbox setup, loading the prompt and the glossaries, the model's time to its first token, generation, the cell on Opus that `cold-read-fast-read.py` runs after the main reader to answer the report's Question 4, "What should be code", and shipping the record to the log-store. `cold-read-fast-read.py` writes no time for each part today; a timestamp at each boundary, written into its record, would give the figures for every later read too.
2. **Ready readers.** Whether a pool of one or two readers started before they are needed, or one reader reused or cleared between reads, would cut that cost, and whether each read would stay independent and fresh, as a cold read must be. One test of freshness: plant a unique phrase in one read's document and check that no later read's report contains it.
3. **Reader models.** A small bake-off of reader models, such as Gemini Flash, Sonnet and the fast Codex model the user mentions, which the owner identifies, on documents with known defects, measuring recall, false alarms and time. The ghi-write candidate defect list, against which the fast read's present reader was chosen (see the docstring of `nc-systems/cold-read/cold-read-fast-read.py`), is one such set.
4. **Which instructions earn their findings.** Have readers name the instruction that raised each finding, and count kept against dropped findings for each instruction, so that instructions that never produce a kept finding can be cut. A kept finding is one the agent applied or brought to the user; a dropped one it set aside in triage. Some instructions keep the reader from false alarms rather than raise findings, and are judged apart.
