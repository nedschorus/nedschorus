# Intro (untitled text under the "# Design review (d-review)" heading)

1. "Review a written document before its content gets expensive: a finished design before anything is built from it, and doctrine before it binds readers." — The instruction is to perform review at a specific timing: review a finished design before any building begins from it, and review doctrine before it becomes binding on the people expected to follow it — the shared rationale being that this is before the document's content becomes costly.
2. "The document must exist and be complete enough to judge — reviewing is judging the written artifact, never co-writing it." — Restates two preconditions (existence, sufficient completeness to judge) and defines "reviewing" as the act of judging the document as a finished object, explicitly excluding any activity that amounts to jointly authoring it.
3. "Review never creates — a finding explains, it does not prescribe (boss-ruled 2026-08-04): a finding makes the defect fully understood — exactly what is wrong, under what conditions it does harm, and why — completely enough that the author can fix it without asking the reviewer anything." — A rule, attributed to a boss ruling dated 2026-08-04: reviewing must never involve creating new content, and each output unit ("a finding") must explain a defect rather than dictate a solution. A finding must make the defect fully understood by covering exactly what is wrong, the conditions under which it causes harm, and why — to a level of completeness such that the author needs no follow-up clarification from the reviewer to fix it.
4. "Designing the fix belongs to the author, and a reviewer who wants to propose an alternative design has left review — that is a create-design task, owned separately." — Responsibility for deciding how to fix something belongs exclusively to the document's author. A reviewer who proposes an alternative design has, by doing so, stepped outside the activity of "review" into a different, separately-owned kind of task ("create-design").
5. "A design that reads as coherent can still rest on an unvalidated assumption, conflate designed with built, lean a load-bearing guarantee on agent discipline, or silently drop a failure mode — defects far cheaper to fix in the document than after the build." — A design can appear internally consistent on a plain read and still contain any of four defects: an unchecked assumption; conflation of "designed" with "built"; a load-bearing guarantee resting on agents voluntarily following a rule rather than on mechanical enforcement; or a silently-omitted failure mode. Each of these four is asserted to be far less costly to fix while still just a document than after something has been built from it.
6. "A shipped instruction file fails differently: one sentence that is ambiguous, self-contradictory, or literally wrong, which a sympathetic reader silently repairs and usually never reports." — A contrasting failure mode specific to already-shipped instruction files: a single sentence can be ambiguous, self-contradictory, or literally incorrect, and a reader inclined toward charitable interpretation will silently fix/reinterpret it in their own head rather than flag it, and will typically never report the problem.
7. "The fixed checklists below keep the review consistent instead of mood-dependent." — Because the checklists that follow are fixed/standardized rather than improvised, using them makes review outcomes consistent across instances, rather than varying with the reviewer's transient state of mind.

3. Rule: an as-yet-unbuilt design gets mode 1; doctrine/instruction files get mode 2; a document that is simultaneously doctrine and a carrier of designed mechanisms gets both modes, run as separate passes rather than combined.

# Steps

1. (Preamble) "Steps 1–4 and 6 apply in both modes; step 5 is discharged by the clarity matrix in mode 2." — Of the six steps, 1–4 and 6 are performed under both modes; step 5 ("get independent passes") is, specifically under mode 2, satisfied by that mode's clarity matrix rather than performed as a separate distinct action.

**Step 1**
2. "Read the whole document, in full — not a skim." — The reviewer must read the entire document completely; partial or superficial reading is explicitly ruled out.
3. "Identify the load-bearing claims: the statements the design depends on being true." — Instructs identifying which statements the design's validity depends on, and defines "load-bearing claims" as exactly those statements.
4. "Those get the hardest scrutiny." — The load-bearing claims just identified are to receive the most rigorous examination relative to other statements.

**Step 2**
5. "Write out your understanding of each mechanism, rule, and load-bearing claim — subtleties elucidated — before hunting defects: edge behavior, boundary conditions, what is not covered, what it does to adjacent state; as exact as the document permits, with unresolved gaps listed as gaps rather than filled by guessing." — Instructs producing a written statement of one's own understanding of every mechanism/rule/load-bearing claim, doing so before beginning defect-hunting (temporal ordering: understanding-writing precedes defect-hunting), making subtleties explicit, and specifically covering edge behavior, boundary conditions, what's not covered, and effects on adjacent state — as precisely as the document allows, recording genuinely unresolved points as explicit gaps rather than guessing a resolution.
6. "A divergence between your written understanding and the document's words is a finding — after ruling out that you simply misread clear text: either the document permitted your misreading, or your correct model contradicts it." — If the reviewer's written understanding diverges from the document's actual words, that divergence is a finding, but only once the possibility of the reviewer having simply misread clear text is ruled out; once ruled out, the divergence must be either that the document's wording was loose enough to permit the misreading, or that the reviewer's understanding is correct and the document's stated words contradict it.
7. "A live specimen of the cost of skipping this: a review once passed the confidently false sentence "uncommitted work has no copy outside the conversation" — a written-out model of the boundary collides with that class of sentence, since on this project's runtime, files on disk survive session restarts." — Gives a real historical example illustrating the cost of skipping step 2: a past review let through a confidently-stated but false sentence claiming uncommitted work has no copy outside the conversation; a written-out model of the relevant boundary would conflict with that class of false sentence, because on this project's actual runtime, disk files persist across session restarts (making the false sentence's claim wrong).
8. "Known limit: if the document and the reviewer are wrong the same way, no restatement reliably detects it; that is what step 4 and the independent passes are for." — Names an acknowledged limitation: when the document's author and the reviewer share the identical mistaken belief, restatement will not reliably catch it (since the restatement merely reproduces the shared error); this is stated to be precisely the purpose of step 4 and the independent passes (step 5).
9. "(This step is invoker-side preparation — in mode 2, the delegated restate cells do their paraphrasing separately and innocently.)" — Clarifies that step 2 is performed by "the invoker" (the orchestrating agent) as its own preparation, distinct from mode 2's delegated "restate cells," which perform their own paraphrasing independently of the invoker's step-2 work and without review-framing influence.

**Step 3**
10. "Run the chosen mode's checklist." — Execute the checklist belonging to whichever mode was selected.
11. "Every finding names the specific location and explains the defect to fix-ready depth — what is wrong, when it does harm, why — never a vague concern, never a proposed fix, and never a severity or importance rating (boss-ruled 2026-08-04): a reviewer without context cannot rate importance, and an out-of-context rating anchors every later reader." — Specifies required content for every finding (specific location; explanation of what's wrong, when it harms, and why, to "fix-ready depth") and three prohibitions (no vague concerns, no proposed fixes, no severity/importance rating — the third attributed to a specific boss ruling), justified by: a reviewer lacking full context cannot judge importance accurately, and an out-of-context rating would unduly bias every subsequent reader who sees it.
12. "A cell states only its own confidence — sure, or unsure and why." — An individual reviewing unit ("cell") may state only its own confidence level in a finding, expressed as either "sure" or "unsure" with a stated reason.
13. "Coverage over self-censorship: report what you are unsure of, with the uncertainty stated; filtering is the synthesis's job." — Prioritizes reporting broadly over withholding uncertain items; things one is unsure of should still be reported with the uncertainty explicitly noted, and deciding what to keep/discard is the responsibility of a later "synthesis" stage, not the reporting reviewer.

**Step 4**
14. "Spot-check the cheap, label the rest — load-bearing claims only (boss-ruled 2026-08-04: verifying everything is overreach)." — Instructs actually verifying claims that are cheap to check, and merely labeling (not verifying) the rest, applying only to load-bearing claims; attributed to a boss ruling reasoning that verifying every claim would be excessive.
15. "When a load-bearing claim can be settled by one command or one file-read — a cited file exists, a quoted line matches, a commit landed — check it: designs' own existence labels are where they lie to themselves (the standing specimen: a backup script everyone believed ran, whose output directory had zero commits in the repository's history)." — Defines "cheap" as settleable by one command or one file read (examples: confirming a cited file exists, a quoted line matches, a commit landed), and instructs checking such claims; justifies this by asserting that existence-claims within designs are the category most prone to self-deception, illustrated by a standing example of a backup script universally believed to have run, whose designated output directory in fact had zero commits in the repository's history.
16. "When checking would be hard, laborious, or impossible, do not attempt it — mark the claim *unverified*, and let that label feed lens 7's measured-or-believed judgment; the label is the deliverable." — For claims that are difficult, effortful, or impossible to check, instructs not attempting verification, instead labeling the claim "unverified," feeding that label into lens 7's later measured/guaranteed/believed classification, and states that applying this label — not a completed verification — is the intended output for such claims.
17. "Exhaustive mechanical checking belongs to code when it is worth doing at all, never to a reviewer's afternoon." — Thorough exhaustive verification should be implemented as automated code (if ever worth building), never performed manually by a reviewer spending time on it.

**Step 5**
18. "Get independent passes." — Instructs obtaining multiple passes over the document, performed independently of one another.
19. "One reader anchored in one context misses what fresh eyes catch, and an author reviewing their own text has the worst of it — the weak parts are already rationalized." — Justifies the prior instruction: a reader fixed within one context fails to notice what a context-free ("fresh") reader would catch, and this problem is most extreme when the reviewer is the text's own author, since the author has already mentally justified the text's weak points, making those weaknesses especially hard for the author to see.
20. "Dispatch fresh subagents against the same document, spawned with nothing but the task (no session context rides along), on each available runtime." — A concrete instruction: launch newly-spawned subagent instances against the same document, each given nothing beyond the task itself (explicitly excluding carryover of the invoking session's context/history), and do this on every currently available runtime.
21. "For a doctrine file, the clarity-review matrix below IS this step." — Clarifies that for doctrine files (mode 2), the later-described clarity-review matrix does not merely resemble but literally constitutes the fulfillment of step 5.

**Step 6**
22. "Write the findings." — Instructs producing the final written record of findings.
23. "The invoker — the one agent holding full context — assigns each finding's severity here, and only here: HIGH = following the words does the wrong thing and the wrongness costs something real; MED = competent readers diverge; LOW = friction, likely recovered." — Identifies "the invoker" as the sole agent with complete context, and states it is this invoker, and only this invoker, who assigns severity, and that this happens at step 6 and only at step 6 (not earlier). Defines: HIGH = literally following the words produces a wrong action whose wrongness has a real/tangible cost; MED = competent readers would nonetheless interpret/act on the text differently from one another; LOW = causes friction but that friction is likely recoverable.
24. "An unconfirmed high-consequence finding is HIGH (unconfirmed), never buried." — A finding that is unconfirmed but would carry high consequence if true must still be labeled HIGH (annotated "(unconfirmed)"), and must never be hidden or deprioritized out of view merely for lacking confirmation.
25. "Order by severity, consequence breaking ties, each explained to fix-ready depth." — Findings must be ordered by severity level, with consequence as the tiebreaker among equal-severity findings, and each finding explained to the "fix-ready depth" standard.
26. "Name what is solid where anything is — and say plainly when nothing is." — The written findings must explicitly identify sound/solid parts of the document wherever any exist, and must state plainly, rather than stay silent, when no part is found to be solid.
27. "End with one line: sound · sound-with-named-risks · not-ready-because-X, where X is the single blocking reason." — The findings must conclude with exactly one line chosen from three fixed options, the third of which is filled in with the single specific reason blocking readiness.

# Mode 1 — the design-soundness checklist

**"Running it" paragraph**
1. "Running it: the lenses fan out — one focused agent per lens or per related group (grouping is the invoker's call), each handed the document and its question; the invoker synthesizes." — Describes the operational procedure: the eleven "lenses" are distributed to multiple agents, either one agent per lens or one per group of related lenses (grouping decided by the invoker), each agent given the document plus its specific assigned question; afterward the invoker combines their outputs.
2. "No single reviewer walks the full lens set inside one context when delegation is available; with no subagent facility, one reviewer working the lenses serially is the accepted degraded mode." — Forbids a single reviewer from covering all eleven lenses within one context whenever delegation is possible, but accepts, as a fallback only when no subagent capability exists at all, one reviewer working through the lenses sequentially.
3. "A re-review always reads the whole document (boss-ruled 2026-08-04) — design defects are global, and an edit collides with unedited text as often as with itself (measured: several of the first full-grid run's catches were changed-versus-unchanged conflicts)." — Attributed to a boss ruling: any subsequent review after revision must still read the entire document, not just changed parts, because design defects are global in nature and an edit conflicts with unedited text about as often as with itself — supported by the measured observation that several catches in the first full-grid run were specifically changed-versus-unchanged conflicts.
4. "What scales with revision size is the reviewer count, never the text scope: a light revision may earn a single good-tier pass over the full document plus verification of each prior finding's fix; a heavy revision earns the full grid." — States that reviewer count, not text scope, is the variable that should scale with revision size (text scope is always the whole document); a light revision merits one "good-tier" pass over the whole document plus a check that each prior finding's fix was actually made; a heavy revision merits running the full grid.
5. "The reviewable unit is the whole file — and doctrine and design files stay small and atomic partly so that whole-file review stays practical." — States that review always operates on an entire file as its unit, and that doctrine/design files are deliberately kept small and self-contained partly because this keeps whole-file review feasible.

**Lens 1 — Unvalidated runtime-boundary claims**
6. "A load-bearing claim resting on first-principles reasoning about how the runtime behaves — what loads when, hook ordering, what a session can and cannot see — needs an empirical probe (or an authoritative contract that specifies the behavior), not assumption." — A load-bearing claim based only on deductive/theoretical reasoning about runtime behavior (examples given: load timing, hook ordering, session visibility) requires either an empirical test or reference to an authoritative specification; assumption alone is insufficient.
7. "Conversely, do not manufacture a probe for a fact true by construction — one that follows from the artifact's own definition, so its falsity would make the mechanism itself pointless." — Conversely, do not force an empirical probe for a fact that is necessarily true given how the thing is defined, such that its falsity would render the mechanism meaningless.
8. "Demand probes for genuine unknowns; stake level is not the discriminator." — Probes should be required for genuinely unknown facts; how much is at stake is explicitly not the criterion for deciding whether a probe is needed.

**Lens 2 — EXISTS-vs-NEW honesty**
9. "Anything labeled as existing that is only designed or proposed (or the reverse) is flagged — the biggest source of design confusion we have observed." — A finding must be raised whenever something labeled as currently existing is actually only designed/proposed, or vice versa; this is asserted to be the largest observed source of design confusion.
10. "Verify each label against ground truth (step 4)." — Each such existence label must be checked against actual fact, with step 4 as the mechanism for doing so.

**Lens 3 — How each rule is backed**
11. "Every load-bearing rule in a design is backed by something: an enforcement point (a gate, a check, a tool boundary) or a written instruction agents follow." — Asserts every load-bearing rule must be backed by either a mechanical enforcement point (examples: gate, check, tool boundary) or a written instruction depending on voluntary agent compliance.
12. "The review checks that the document says which, truthfully: a claimed mechanism must actually exist (lens 2 verifies it), and a written instruction must be labeled as what it is." — Instructs verifying the document truthfully states which backing type applies: a claimed enforcement mechanism must actually exist (verified via lens 2), and a mere written instruction must be honestly labeled as such.
13. "The finding is discipline presented as enforcement — a false claim about how the rule is backed." — Names the target finding of this lens: something that is actually discipline (voluntary compliance) being falsely presented as if it were enforcement.
14. "A design may freely specify what becomes code and what stays a written instruction; those are ordinary design decisions." — A design is permitted, without restriction, to decide which rules become enforced code and which remain written instructions, and such decisions are categorized as ordinary/unremarkable.
15. "Re-evaluating that split — "this prompt rule should be code," or the reverse — is a different problem set, out of this review's scope (boss-ruled 2026-08-04; the question lives per-candidate on [the what-can-code-check issue, nedschorus#42]) — unless the code-versus-prompts choice is itself the subject of the document or section under review, in which case it is reviewed like any other design decision." — Second-guessing which rules should be code versus prompt-based belongs to a separate problem category and is excluded from this review's scope (attributed to a boss ruling, with that question instead handled per-candidate via a specific referenced issue) — except when the code-versus-prompt choice is itself what the document or section under review is actually about, in which case it is reviewed normally.

**Lens 4 — Gaps and silently-dropped cases**
16. "For each mechanism, walk the state space systematically: actor states (busy, idle, mid-turn, dead), dependency failures (the file it reads, the tool it runs, the channel it writes), concurrency (two sessions, re-entry, repeated firing) — and require the document to name or explicitly discard the reachable, consequential cells." — For every mechanism, instructs systematically enumerating possible states across three categories (actor states, dependency failures, concurrency scenarios, each with named examples) and requires the document to address — by naming or explicitly discarding — every reachable and consequential combination.
17. "The enumeration prompts the reviewer; it does not bind the document to a transcript of every cell." — Clarifies the enumeration exists to guide the reviewer's thinking, and does not require the document itself to explicitly write up every single combination.
18. "A reachable, relevant omission is a finding even when the happy path is flawless — the best catches live in cells the design's own story never visits." — A missing reachable/consequential combination is a finding regardless of whether the normal-case description is flawless; asserts the most valuable findings tend to come from combinations the document's own narrative never considers.

**Lens 5 — Over-complexity**
19. "Machinery whose value does not justify its cost." — Definitional fragment: this lens targets machinery whose benefit does not justify its cost.
20. "Two named smells: *unnecessary tracked state* — state the design maintains that a state-agnostic mechanism would obviate; and a *compensating mechanism* — machinery papering over a gap that a simpler primitive would eliminate outright." — Defines two patterns: "unnecessary tracked state" (state kept by the design that a state-independent mechanism would make unnecessary) and "compensating mechanism" (machinery working around a gap that a simpler basic building block would eliminate entirely).
21. "The finding names the simpler mechanism that suffices — not as a proposal, but as the evidence that the machinery is over-complex." — A finding from this lens must name the simpler sufficient mechanism, but this naming serves only as evidence that the existing machinery is over-complex, not as a proposed replacement.

**Lens 6 — Internal consistency**
22. "The document contradicting itself, its own stated principles, or the project's recorded rulings — the governing plan documents and issue bodies, which the lens agent is given alongside the document." — Defines this lens's scope: contradictions between the document and itself, its own stated principles, or the project's recorded rulings (identified as governing plan documents and issue bodies), and notes the lens agent is given these reference materials in addition to the target document.
23. "Internal inconsistency is strong evidence of an unexamined call; confirm the exception is not deliberate before flagging." — Asserts internal inconsistency strongly suggests a decision was made without careful examination, but instructs first confirming the apparent inconsistency is not an intentional/deliberate exception before reporting it as a finding.

**Lens 7 — Reliability grounding**
24. "Each load-bearing mechanism is measured (probe, canary, field observation), guaranteed (by definition or an authoritative contract — lens 1's by-construction class), or merely believed." — Asserts a three-way classification every load-bearing mechanism must fall into: measured (via probe, canary, or field observation), guaranteed (true by definition or authoritative contract, referring back to lens 1's "true by construction" category), or merely believed.
25. "Believed plus load-bearing is a named risk until measured." — A mechanism that is both load-bearing and merely believed (not measured or guaranteed) must be explicitly flagged as a risk, and remains so until it is measured.

**Lens 8 — Build-order sanity**
26. "Does the order remove the highest live risk first — ranked by probability times the cost of late discovery?" — Poses whether the build order addresses the highest-ranked risk first, where rank is computed as probability multiplied by the cost of late discovery.
27. "Is the highest-value piece scheduled sensibly or buried behind lower-value work?" — Poses whether the single most valuable piece is positioned sensibly in the order, or scheduled after lower-value pieces.

**Lens 9 — Scale and growth**
28. "Potentially unbounded data the design accumulates needs a stated bound — retention, archival, or the project's artifact-lifecycle rule that every accumulating store has a named home and a drain — plus a rough volume expectation." — Any data the design accumulates that could grow without limit must have an explicit bound (acceptable forms: retention policy, archival policy, or the project's general rule that every accumulating store has a named permanent location and a drain mechanism), plus a rough estimate of expected volume.
29. "Unbounded growth with correctness-only thinking is a default blind spot; flag the missing bound even when the ceiling looks far off." — Asserts that considering only correctness (and not growth) tends by default to overlook this issue, and instructs flagging a missing bound even when the point of actual problem appears distant.

**Lens 10 — Test-plan completeness**
30. "A plan that maps the design's own cells is necessary but, unless it includes generative techniques (fuzzing, property tests), only exercises what the design thought of." — A test plan covering the design's own explicitly-considered scenarios is necessary but, absent generative techniques (examples: fuzzing, property tests), only tests what was already anticipated.
31. "Where the designed thing has an executable surface, require a second, adversarial layer — load, scale, "what did we not anticipate" — that does not assume the design is right." — Wherever the designed thing has something executable, requires an additional adversarial testing layer (examples: load testing, scale testing, unanticipated-scenario testing) that does not presuppose the design's correctness.

**Lens 11 — Naming**
32. "A name, for this lens, is an identifier that anything outside the document will use to find or invoke the thing (boss-scoped 2026-08-04): file and directory names; script, function, and command names; skill names; issue and test labels; defined terms other documents will cite." — Gives a scoped definition (attributed to a boss decision) of "name" as any identifier used by something external to the document to locate or invoke the referent, listing examples: file/directory names, script/function/command names, skill names, issue/test labels, and terms defined here that other documents will cite.
33. "Ordinary prose vocabulary, one-off words, and local labels defined where they are used (a tier scheme, a severity scale) are not names and are out of this lens's scope." — Excludes three categories from the "name" definition: ordinary prose vocabulary, words used only once, and labels defined locally at their point of use (examples: a tier scheme, a severity scale).
34. "Every in-scope name must be self-documenting and greppable: full words a search matches verbatim, one shared token across a family of related names, no cryptic abbreviations, no bare sequence labels, and no bare numeric issue references in prose (the number must ride with a descriptive handle)." — Requires in-scope names to be self-documenting and text-searchable: composed of full words matchable verbatim, sharing one common token across related-name families, free of cryptic abbreviations, free of bare sequence labels, and requiring a bare issue number in prose to always be accompanied by a descriptive label.
35. "Expect almost every truly self-documenting name to run two to five words (boss calibration 2026-08-03): a one-word name is a finding candidate by default when the word is generic or collides in context (`parser`, `data`, `manager`); domain-standard tokens (`README`, `checksum`, `SHA-256`) pass." — Attributed to a boss calibration: expects self-documenting names to typically be two to five words long; a one-word name is by default a candidate finding when generic/context-ambiguous (examples given); but domain-standard single-word tokens (examples given) are exempted and pass.
36. "A longer precise name beats a short ambiguous one, and ease of typing is not a constraint." — States a preference for longer-but-precise over shorter-but-ambiguous names, and explicitly excludes typing convenience as a legitimate factor in that decision.
37. "A bad name in a design propagates into code, tests, and doctrine — flag it here, where the rename is still cheap." — Justifies flagging names at design-review time: a poor name will carry forward into code, tests, and doctrine, and renaming is cheapest at this design stage.

# Mode 2 — the clarity review (doctrine and instruction files)

1. "The failure mode is sentence-level: a reader who cannot follow a sentence without guessing, or two readers who follow it into different behavior." — Defines the specific failure this mode targets as occurring at the level of individual sentences, manifesting either as one reader needing to guess at meaning, or as two readers each interpreting the same sentence into differing behavior.
2. "Two pass types, and they run in SEPARATE agents — never one agent doing both: a single agent doing one task first is primed for the second (a defect-hunt frame makes its restatement adversarial; a restatement frame makes its hunt post-hoc)." — States there are two pass types (restatement, defect-hunt), mandates they be run by separate agents (never the same agent doing both), and gives the reason: an agent that performs one task first becomes biased for the second — a prior defect-hunt makes a subsequent restatement adversarial rather than innocent, and a prior restatement makes a subsequent defect-hunt merely retroactive justification rather than a genuinely fresh hunt.

**Pass 1 — Restatement pass**
3. "The cell's prompt carries no review framing — only the paraphrase template and the target path, nothing else." — The instructions given to a restatement-pass agent must exclude any hint that it is participating in a review, containing only the paraphrase template and the document path.
4. "The finding is not the paraphrase — it is the **divergence** between the paraphrase and the intended meaning." — Clarifies that what counts as a finding is not the paraphrase text itself, but the difference between the paraphrase and the document's actually intended meaning.
5. "An innocent paraphrase often exposes ambiguity by misreading it." — Asserts that a non-adversarial paraphrase frequently reveals ambiguity precisely because the paraphrasing agent unintentionally misreads the ambiguous text.

**Pass 2 — Defect-hunt pass**
6.
