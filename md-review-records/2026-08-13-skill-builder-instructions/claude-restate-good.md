## How skills are built here

1. **"Find the **skill-authoring checklist** under `docs/` and follow it; it is the project's own standard and predates you."** — Somewhere inside the `docs/` directory there is a document called the skill-authoring checklist; its exact path is deliberately not given, so locating it is part of your task. Once found, comply with it. It carries authority for two stated reasons: it is the project's own established standard (not something imported or invented for you), and it existed before this seat did — implying it is not yours to rewrite or override.

2. **"The existing skills are the worked examples — `.claude/skills/walk-me-through/`, `.claude/skills/md-review/`, `.claude/skills/handoff/`, `.claude/skills/ghi-write/` — and reading two of them closely is the cheapest way to learn the house style."** — Skills already built and living in the repository serve as fully worked reference examples of what a finished skill looks like; four are named by directory path. Reading any two of them carefully (not all four, and no particular two are specified) is the least-effort route to absorbing the "house style" — this project's local conventions for how skill text is written.

3. **"Three rules that have bitten previous builds:"** — What follows is a list of three rules. "Bitten" means they have caused unexpected trouble for earlier attempts at building skills — either earlier builds violated them and paid for it, or the rules tripped those builds up unexpectedly. The point of listing them is that they are the known failure points.

4. **"1. **A skill is instruction-class.**"** — A skill file falls into the project's category of "instruction" files (as distinct from, say, code or ordinary documentation), and that classification is what subjects it to the stricter handling described in the next sentence.

5. **"It lands only through the user's walk, and the `instruction-file-guard` hook enforces that mechanically — a `.walk-approved` marker quoting his approval, consumed by the one write it approves."** — "Lands" means gets written into the repository as a committed artifact. The only permitted route for that is the user being walked through the content and approving it. This is not left to the agent's self-discipline: a hook named `instruction-file-guard` enforces it automatically at the tooling level. The enforcement mechanism works via a marker file named `.walk-approved` whose contents include a quotation of the user's own approving words; that marker is consumed — used up and thereby invalidated — by the single write operation it authorises, so one approval cannot be stretched to cover a second write.

6. **"2. **Skills are instructions, not essays.**"** — The genre a skill file belongs to is directive text addressed to an actor, not expository or argumentative prose written to be read and considered.

7. **"Rationale asides get cut; the text tells an agent what to do."** — Passages that explain the reasoning behind an instruction, inserted as side remarks, are removed during editing. What remains is text whose function is to direct an agent's actions.

8. **"Four such asides were cut from `walk-me-through` on 2026-08-06 for exactly this reason."** — A concrete precedent: on 6 August 2026, four rationale asides of that kind were deleted from the `walk-me-through` skill, and the motivation for deleting them was precisely the rule just stated.

9. **"3. **Zero-context readability is the bar.**"** — The standard a skill must clear is that it be readable and comprehensible by someone who arrives with no surrounding context at all — none of the conversation, history, or shared background that produced it.

10. **"The user ruled 2026-08-11 that agents must understand their instructions cold, which is why a settled skill draft gets an md-review before it lands."** — On 11 August 2026 the user made a binding decision (a "ruling," treated as settled and not to be relitigated) that agents have to be able to understand their instructions "cold" — on first encounter, with no warm-up or prior familiarity. The stated consequence of that ruling: once a skill draft has stopped changing ("settled" = the content is stable, not still in flux), it must go through an md-review before being committed.
